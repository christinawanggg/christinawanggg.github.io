---
title: one hop further
date: 2026-09-01
---

friction is underpriced. the best places, ideas, and career moves usually sit one inconvenient step past where everyone else decided "good enough" was good enough. i see this same pattern show up in how i travel and in how i think about work.

## 2 hours past cancun

cancun is the hop everyone takes. it's popular for a reason: good weather, good beaches, easy flights. but popularity has a cost. the longer a place has been on everyone's list, the more crowded, expensive, and stripped of its original character it gets.

holbox is two hours past cancun. whale sharks, flamingos, water that lights up at night from bioluminescence. affordable, quiet, exactly what a relaxing trip is supposed to feel like. most people never make it there, not because it's hard to find, but because it takes an extra two hours and a little more research than booking the resort everyone already knows about.

same pattern with ericeira, tucked just off the side of lisbon and sintra. same with utila, one ferry past roatan. these three are some of my favorite places i've ever traveled to, and it's not a coincidence. the good version of the trip is rarely hidden. it's just slightly less convenient to reach, and that's usually enough to keep most people from bothering.

## hatch

i interned at hatch in college, right at the start of covid. hatch made a night light and sound machine for babies. by the time i got there, it was already in something like 1 in 4 families with a newborn. that's a real business. most companies would have kept mining that market forever, and no one would have blamed them.

instead they added a new product line for adults and started pouring real focus into it. an alarm clock for grown-ups felt like a strange move to a 20 year old, sleep deprived college student watching it happen from the inside. babies were the obvious market, the one already proven. adults were the unproven, harder sell, a wellness pitch to people who'd never thought about their sunrise alarm as a category. i joined a month after the first adult clock launched and didn't really get why they were taking focus away from something already working.

i get it now. adults turned into the bigger, stickier market, and hatch is mostly known for the adult product today. i use it every day now and can't imagine my life without it. the pivot in focus that looked unnecessary from inside a college internship looks obvious in hindsight, the same way 2 hours past cancun looks obvious once you've actually been.

## the pattern

the same pattern shows up across the best startups. uber is the clean version: paying to get into a stranger's car felt reckless when it launched, not a business, a liability. now we don't think twice about it anywhere in the world. the idea wasn't hidden. it was one uncomfortable step past what everyone already accepted as normal, and almost nobody was willing to take that step first.

the crowd stops at cancun. the crowd keeps mining the market that's already proven. the opportunity was never really hidden, it was just one hop further than most people were willing to go, whether that hop is a two-hour ferry or a harder, less obvious market to build for.
